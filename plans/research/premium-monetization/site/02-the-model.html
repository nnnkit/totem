02 · The Model

The Premium Model & Why It Fits

Take Totem from free + open-source to a freemium product with a $19 one-time "Pro" unlock — without breaking the local-first, no-server, open-source ethos that is the brand. Grounded in a 9-agent deep dive of the actual codebase plus the 2026 payments/extension market. Date: 2026-06-19.

The four decisions

  1. Model: Freemium, one build, runtime-gated. The free tier stays fully functional forever; Pro is a one-time lifetime unlock. This is the only model the pipeline (single artifact, single CWS listing) and the audience (subscription-averse, anti-DRM) actually support.
  2. The work is packaging, not building. 5 of the 6 proposed Pro features already exist and ship for free today — export, deleted-tweet preservation (the hydration engine), advanced search filters, annotations, thread-aware capture. Only bulk ops is net-new. The real engineering is a single clean entitlement boundary, not new features.
  3. Payments: a merchant-of-record (Lemon Squeezy or Polar) for checkout, so global VAT/tax is handled and there's no Totem billing server. Verify with offline Ed25519-signed license keys (@noble/ed25519, which pairs with the @noble/hashes already in the repo) so the extension makes zero license network calls — "no server, works offline, nothing can shut down" stays literally true once activated (checkout itself still needs the MoR online).
  4. The #1 risk is the retroactive paywall. Totem is already free; bolting a paywall onto existing users generates 1-star reviews. Grandfather every existing install (full features, free, forever) and only ever gate new installs. This single move neutralizes the biggest execution risk.
CORE BET
One artifact, gated at runtime; one-time lifetime price; offline-verifiable keys; never claw back from existing users.

The premium model (and why this one)

The model is not a preference — it's forced by four hard constraints, two from the code and two from the audience. Each one points the same direction.

ConstraintWhat it forces
Pipeline is single-artifact (one dist/, one zip, one CWS item, version-pinned)One build, entitlement-gated at runtime. Not separate Free/Pro builds.
Audience = r/nosurf, r/selfhosted, HN, PKM (subscription-fatigued, anti-DRM, pro-ownership)One-time "lifetime" unlock, Obsidian-Catalyst-style "fund the work," not a subscription.
Local-first, no server, no telemetry is the entire pitchVerification must be local/offline; no per-launch phone-home.
Open-source (code is public on GitHub)The check is patchable by design — accept it, out-serve the cracker, don't build DRM.

The 2026 market evidence (first-party)

The one-time model isn't just on-brand — it converts better for this product category. The first-party signals from 2026:

  • PaletteGrab got a 60% conversion lift switching from monthly to one-time.
  • MiroMiro got "5 paying customers in a row" after adding a lifetime option.
  • The consensus from extension builders: "Chrome extensions aren't SaaS apps. They're tools. People prefer to buy tools once."
Subscriptions still win high-revenue, server-cost categories — SEO, dev-tools, AI at $5k–200k/mo. But Totem is squarely a low-marginal-cost client utility, the bucket where one-time wins.

Realistic conversion to model against: 1–3% of active users (not installs), at roughly 60–70% checkout completion.

Pricing

  • $19 one-time lifetime — the GTM-validated number: above the $4.99 utility floor, below the $29 "enterprise-parity" expectation.
  • $14 founders price for the first 60 days, framed honestly ("$14 founders price, rising to $19") — no fake countdowns.
  • Never "2 years of updates." Use "lifetime," no expiry qualifier — the local-first architecture means it genuinely works forever.
PRICE
$19 lifetime, $14 founders for 60 days. Honest framing, no countdown theater, no expiry on "lifetime."
