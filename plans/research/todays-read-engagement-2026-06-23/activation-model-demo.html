What the science actually says

19 agents across 8 evidence lenses, then a feasibility pass and an adversarial audit that fact-checked the research against its own citations. The audit's job was to catch over-claiming — and it did. Here is the unvarnished read.

The single best-powered study in this whole brief is a deflationary one. Maier et al. (2022, PNAS) re-analysed the nudge literature for publication bias and found the average effect collapses from d≈0.43 to d≈0.04–0.11 — and pure-information nudges to roughly zero. So the honest planning assumption is that every activation tactic here is worth at most +2 to +5 percentage points on handled-rate, and might be worth nothing. That changes the strategy: ship calm-by-default as the product truth (not as a promised conversion machine), and gate every warming/breathing/gauge tactic behind a pre-registered A/B that the retention guardrails are allowed to kill.

The five things we don't actually know

If we ship all of this and measure honestly, these are the bets most likely to be wrong. Naming them is the point — it's what keeps "science fact" from quietly becoming "science vibes."

  1. ① The "Not for me" release might lower real reading, not raise completion. It's the mechanic the whole success metric leans on, and it has zero direct evidence. A one-tap exit could just become a faster way to clear the card without reading. This is the highest-priority experiment.
  2. ② The draining gauge could read as pressure. "Minutes left" shrinking toward zero motivates people who want to finish — and, per the same source, slows people who'd rather prolong. We don't know which Totem users are. Drain-vs-fill-vs-dots is a real test, not a chosen answer.
  3. ③ The wind-down cue is real but small (~0.15, not 0.65). Implementation intentions are the best-replicated tool in self-regulation, but the headline number is lab-inflated, and our version is a single dismissible note rather than a rehearsed plan. Plan for modest.
  4. ④ The dusk color does the least. No study links warm tint to finishing a reading set, and warm hue trends toward arousal, not calm. It earns its place only as a wordless, redundant reinforcement of the gauge — and only if the arousal guardrails stay green.
  5. ⑤ A forgiving rhythm may not lift reading at all. The evidence is almost entirely about loss-framed streaks (which we're deliberately not building). We keep it because avoiding the harm is free, not because the gentle version is proven to help.

How we'll know it worked (and pre-commit to it)

Because every lever is individually weak, the measurement design is the actual product. Pre-register each metric's "good direction" before the test, so a tempting read-rate bump can't retroactively redefine success.

Primary% of today's set HANDLED = (read OR consciously released) ÷ set size, per user per day.
Secondary & guardShare actually read (scroll-depth / dwell). Kept separate from the primary as a cannibalisation guard — if the release valve or the gauge lifts "handled" but this drops materially, the win is vetoed.
Retention vetoesUninstall rate (hard veto) · activation snooze/dismiss rate · new-tab-open frequency decline over 7 days · time-on-newtab with a pre-registered direction (for a finish-and-leave product, lower is often the healthy signal — a rise can mean the card is pulling people off their real work).
Non-negotiableReduced-motion users get a static warm tint / instant fallback. Not an A/B — an accessibility floor.
ExperimentsA1 gauge direction (drain vs fill vs dots) · A2 release-valve cannibalisation (present vs absent, co-primary with share-read) · A3 activation (calm vs warm-tint vs warm+breathing) · A4 wind-down framing (if-then vs neutral label) · A5 forgiving rhythm (opt-in cohort vs none). All on a 4-week window to catch slow-burn trust cost.
