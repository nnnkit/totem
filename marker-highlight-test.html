On the morning of the regime change

Multi-color demo: the three pre-placed highlights use different colors. Add your own.

A whole civilization will die tonight, never to be brought back again. I don't want that to happen, but it probably will. However, now that we have Complete and Total Regime Change, where everything is different than it was before, maybe we stand a chance.

The morning sky had the color of a bruise that's been left too long — purple at the center, yellowing out toward the edges. Birds flew in patterns nobody recognized. Across the valley, the old towers were being disassembled one story at a time, not dramatically, just patiently, like someone undoing a knot they'd been studying for years.

Nobody had told the children yet. Nobody had told the people who work the early shift at the bakery, who were already pulling trays out of ovens that ran on the old schedule, timed to a world that was about to be replaced with a different world running on a different clock. The bread would come out fine. It always does.

Later, someone would write that the day felt ordinary until it didn't — which is how these things always feel, apparently, and which is the kind of sentence that appears in every book about every such day, and which never quite captures it.
